# THE STRATA STORY
### A simple guide for owners — what's mine, what's "ours", and who pays when something breaks
*Based on NSW Strata Legislation · Building Commission NSW · 369 Alliance*

---

## How to read this guide

This is a story, not a law book. Every chapter answers ONE question that owners ask us all the time. At the end of every chapter you will find:

- **The rule in one line.**
- **Who pays.**
- **The law behind it.**

If you only have 5 minutes, read **Chapter 2** (the imaginary line) and **Chapter 5** (the decision tree). Those two answer 80% of the calls we get.

# CHAPTER 1 — What is Strata, really?

Imagine 50 families decide to live in the same building. Every family wants their own front door, their own kitchen, their own bedroom. But everybody has to share the lift, the roof, the driveway, the walls between apartments, and the pipes inside the walls.

Now imagine each family also has to share **the cost of fixing those shared things** — forever.

That is Strata.

When you buy an apartment in NSW, you are not buying just an apartment. You are buying **two things at once**:

1. **Your Lot** — the box of air inside your apartment.
2. **A share of the Common Property** — a slice of everything that is shared.

Together with all the other owners, you automatically become a member of a club called **the Owners Corporation**. You did not sign up. You cannot resign. The day you got the keys, you joined.

> **Rule in one line:** Buying an apartment means buying a private box + a share of a shared building.
> **Who pays:** You pay levies (your share of the bills) every quarter, for as long as you own.
> **The law:** *Strata Schemes Management Act 2015* (SSMA) and *Strata Schemes Development Act 2015* (SSDA).

# CHAPTER 2 — The Imaginary Line (the most important thing in this guide)

There is a **line** that runs around the inside of every apartment. You cannot see it, but it is on the **Strata Plan** that was registered when the building was created.

- Everything on **YOUR side** of the line = **Lot Property** = **YOUR responsibility.**
- Everything on **THE OTHER side** of the line = **Common Property** = **the Owners Corporation's responsibility.**

This is the rule for every strata scheme registered after **1 July 1974** (which is almost every modern apartment).

### What is on YOUR side of the line (Lot Property)

- Light fittings, light switches, power points, blinds and curtains.
- Internal walls **not shown on the Strata Plan** (the walls inside your apartment).
- Floor finishes (carpet, floorboards, the tiles you laid yourself).
- Kitchen cupboards, benchtops, ovens, dishwashers, all your appliances.
- Tiles and waterproofing **on internal walls** not shown on the Strata Plan.
- Bath, basin, toilet, taps, and any plumbing **above the upper surface of the floor**.

### What is on THE OTHER side of the line (Common Property)

- The **structure** — floor slabs, ceilings, columns, beams.
- **External windows, external doors, balcony doors** (the ones you see from the street).
- The **original floor tiles**, including the original tiles in the shower.
- The **original waterproofing membrane** under those tiles.
- Tiles and waterproofing **on boundary walls** (the walls you share with your neighbour or with the corridor).
- The **structural cubic space** — meaning:
  - shared plumbing,
  - the voids that hide shared plumbing and wires,
  - any wiring outside your apartment or shared between apartments.

> **Rule in one line:** If it was built originally and serves more than one owner, it is Common Property.
> **Who pays:** Lot side = the owner. Common side = all owners (through levies).
> **The law:** *Strata Schemes Development Act 2015*, Schedule 2, plus your registered Strata Plan.

# CHAPTER 3 — The Cast (who is who in your building)

Strata has more characters than a Netflix series. Knowing who does what saves you years of frustration.

| Character | What they actually do | Can they decide on remedial works? |
|-----------|----------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **The Owner (you)** | Owns one Lot + share of Common Property. Pays levies. Votes at meetings. | Only on what is inside your own Lot. |
| **The Owners Corporation (OC)** | Legal "company" made up of ALL owners. Owns the Common Property. | **YES — this is the only body that can sign a remediation contract.** |
| **The Strata Committee** | A small group of owners (3–9) elected to handle day-to-day decisions. | Some, within authority limits set by the OC. Big jobs go back to the OC. |
| **The Strata Manager** | A licensed agent hired to run admin, levies, meetings, insurance. | **NO.** Cannot sign remedial works contracts without OC approval. |
| **The Building Manager** | Hired to look after the physical building (cleaning, gardens, small repairs). | **NO.** Cannot sign remedial works contracts. |
| **NCAT (the Tribunal)** | Resolves disputes that the OC cannot fix internally. | Decides disputes; orders works in some cases. |
| **NSW Fair Trading** | Free mediation for strata disputes before NCAT. | No — they mediate. |
| **Building Commission NSW** | The regulator. Inspects works. Issues fines. Cancels practitioner registrations. | They police; they don't decide your project. |

> **Rule in one line:** Only the Owners Corporation can authorise major remedial works. Not the strata manager. Not the building manager. Not one loud owner.
> **The law:** *Strata Schemes Management Act 2015*, sections 5, 8, 9 and 106.

# CHAPTER 4 — The Rule Book (which laws actually apply to you)

Eight laws control your apartment life. You do not need to read them — you just need to know **which one to point at** when something happens.

| When this happens… | The law you point at |
|---|---|
| Meetings, levies, by-laws, voting, repairs of common property | **Strata Schemes Management Act 2015** (SSMA) + Regulation 2016 |
| The strata plan itself, subdivision, ending the scheme | **Strata Schemes Development Act 2015** (SSDA) + Regulation 2016 |
| Designs and builders for waterproofing, cladding, fire, structure on Class 2 buildings | **Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020** (DBP Act) + Regulation 2021 |
| Defects and serious problems in residential apartment buildings | **Residential Apartment Buildings (Compliance and Enforcement Powers) Act 2020** (RAB Act) |
| Builder warranties (2-year minor / 6-year major) on work since 1989 | **Home Building Act 1989** (HBA) |
| Whether the work needs council approval (DA, CDC, exempt) | **Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979** + **Codes SEPP 2008** |
| The strata manager's licence and conduct | **Property and Stock Agents Act 2002** |
| Buying or selling the apartment | **Conveyancing Act 1919** |

> **Rule in one line:** Day-to-day = SSMA. Building quality = DBP + RAB. Defects warranty = HBA. Approval needed? = EPA Act + Codes SEPP.

# CHAPTER 5 — The Decision Tree (whose problem is it?)

Use this BEFORE you call anyone. It will save you weeks of arguing.

```
  Something just broke or leaked.
              │
              ▼
  Is the broken thing on the
  LOT side of the imaginary line?
  (light fitting, your dishwasher,
   the tiles you renovated yourself,
   plumbing above the floor surface)
              │
        ┌─────┴─────┐
       YES          NO
        │           │
        ▼           ▼
  YOU pay.    Is it ORIGINAL to the building?
  YOU fix.    (original tiles, original
              waterproofing, structure,
              external window, shared pipe)
                    │
              ┌─────┴─────┐
             YES          NO
              │           │
              ▼           ▼
       OWNERS CORP    Whoever caused it
       pays & fixes.  / installed it pays
       (Common Prop)  & fixes.
```

### The 3 questions that almost always resolve it

1. **Where is it?** (inside the line or outside?)
2. **Was it there from day 1?** (original = usually Common Property)
3. **Does it serve more than one apartment?** (yes = Common Property)

> **Rule in one line:** Original + serves more than one Lot = Owners Corporation. Yours alone, installed by you, above the floor = you.
> **The law:** *SSMA 2015* s.106 (duty of OC to maintain common property) + Schedule 2 of *SSDA 2015* (definition of common property).

# CHAPTER 6 — The Remediation Story (what happens when something BIG breaks)

Big = the roof leaks across 6 apartments. The façade is cracking. The basement waterproofing failed. The cladding has to come off.

These are **remedial building works** and they have their own rulebook now (since 2020).

### Step 1 — Spot it
Anyone can spot it: an owner, the building manager, an inspector. The issue is reported to the **Strata Committee** or the **Strata Manager**.

### Step 2 — Get advice
If nobody knows whether council approval is needed, the Owners Corporation engages a **Planning Consultant** (the Planning Institute of Australia keeps a register).

The Planning Consultant tells you one of three things:
- **Exempt Development** — go ahead, no DA needed (but DBP rules may still apply, see Step 4).
- **Complying Development Certificate (CDC)** — fast-track approval needed.
- **Development Application (DA)** — full council approval needed.

### Step 3 — The Owners Corporation VOTES
Nothing happens until the OC passes a motion at a general meeting. **The strata manager cannot sign. The building manager cannot sign. One owner cannot sign.** The whole OC must agree.

### Step 4 — The DBP twist (the most misunderstood rule in NSW)
For **Class 2 buildings** (apartment buildings), even works that don't need council approval might still need:
- A **Registered Design Practitioner** to prepare a **Construction-Issued Regulated Design** + a **Design Compliance Declaration**.
- A **Registered Building Practitioner** to actually build it + a **Building Compliance Declaration** at the end.

**Two famous exceptions that catch everyone out:**

| Type of work | Is DBP usually triggered? |
|---|---|
| **Waterproofing** | YES. Unless ALL three apply: (1) it's because of a bathroom/kitchen/laundry/toilet alteration, AND (2) it's only one single dwelling, AND (3) it's exempt development. |
| **External cladding** | YES. Unless the work is **not** being done because of a council order or development control order. If there is an order, DBP applies. |

> Even if your work is "exempt development" under the Codes SEPP, **waterproofing and cladding still need a Registered Design Practitioner.**

### Step 5 — Lodge designs on the NSW Planning Portal
The Registered Building Practitioner uploads:
- The construction-issued regulated designs.
- The design compliance declaration.
- The contractor's public liability + contract works insurance.

**Nothing on site can start before this lodgement is complete.**

### Step 6 — Build
The Registered Building Practitioner must build **exactly to the lodged designs**. If something has to change, that becomes a **Variation** — a new design + new declaration is lodged on the Portal during the works.

### Step 7 — Close out
At the end, the Registered Building Practitioner lodges a **Building Compliance Declaration** on the Portal, plus:
- a **Variation Statement** (if any variations happened),
- a **Contractor Document** (list of contractors used).

This is the only document that **cannot be delegated**. Only the registered builder can sign it. Then the case is closed on the Portal.

> **Rule in one line:** Owners vote → designer designs → portal upload → builder builds → builder declares → case closed.
> **The law:** *DBP Act 2020*, *DBP Regulation 2021* (clause 13 has the exclusion list), *RAB Act 2020*, and *Codes SEPP 2008*.

# CHAPTER 7 — The "It's an Emergency" Exception

Sometimes you cannot wait for designs. The roof is open. People can't sleep in their apartment. There is real risk to safety.

This is **Emergency Remedial Building Work**. The rules bend — but only a little.

The Owners Corporation must engage a **Registered Building Practitioner** to confirm in writing that there is a "**reasonable excuse**". To qualify, ALL of these must be true:

1. **Immediate** action is needed.
2. The issue is causing or likely to cause damage.
3. It impacts: ability to live in the building, OR health and safety, OR risk of further damage.
4. The impact is **serious**.
5. The work done is **only what is necessary** to mitigate it — not the full final fix.

If all five apply, the builder can start **without a regulated design**. But within **7 days** of finishing the emergency work, they must lodge on the NSW Planning Portal:
- A registration of the emergency work.
- A Building Compliance Declaration.

If the emergency work does not actually meet the BCA, the builder must say so on the declaration form. The OC then has to plan the proper remedial works to follow.

> **Rule in one line:** Emergency = act now, declare within 7 days, then plan the proper fix.
> **The law:** *DBP Act 2020* — emergency remedial building work provisions.

# CHAPTER 8 — The Penalty Story (why this is not optional)

Owners often say: "Can we just skip the paperwork and get on with it?"

No. Here is what the Building Commission NSW can do:

| What went wrong | Penalty for a body corporate | Penalty for an individual |
|---|---|---|
| Registered Design Practitioner failed to provide design compliance declarations | Up to **$165,000** | Up to **$55,000** |
| Registered Building Practitioner failed to lodge the declared designs | Up to **$33,000** | Up to **$11,000** |
| Significant offences | Prosecution | Prosecution |

On top of fines, the Building Commission NSW can issue:
- **Stop Work Orders** under the RAB Act.
- **Building Work Rectification Orders** — forcing the OC to undo and redo the work.
- **Prohibition Orders** — stopping the issue of the Occupation Certificate or registration of a strata plan (for new buildings).

These orders sit on the public register. Future buyers will see them.

> **Rule in one line:** Cutting corners on DBP costs more than doing it properly.
> **The law:** *DBP Act 2020* + *RAB Act 2020*.

# CHAPTER 9 — "But what about my renovation?"

Renovations inside your Lot are governed by the **SSMA 2015** (sections 108–110). The law puts your work into one of three buckets:

| Type | What it means | What you need |
|---|---|---|
| **Cosmetic Work** (s.109) | Painting, hanging pictures, putting up curtains, replacing handles, installing a TV or hooks. | **Nothing.** Just go ahead. |
| **Minor Renovations** (s.110) | New kitchen, new built-in wardrobe, new internal walls, recessed lights, hardwood/laminate flooring. | **Owners Corporation approval by ordinary resolution** (50%+ at a meeting). |
| **Major Renovations** | Anything that changes the external appearance, touches **structure**, **waterproofing**, **fire safety**, or **common property**. | **By-law** approved by **special resolution** (75%+) — and now usually a DBP-compliant designer + builder. |

**Trap:** Replacing your bathroom waterproofing membrane is NOT a cosmetic job. It touches the original waterproofing — which is Common Property. You need OC approval AND a Registered Design Practitioner.

> **Rule in one line:** If it changes the look from outside, touches structure, fire, or waterproofing — it's Major. Get a by-law and a registered practitioner.
> **The law:** *SSMA 2015* sections 108–110, plus *DBP Act 2020* for waterproofing.

# CHAPTER 10 — The First-Year Story (when the building is brand new)

When a new building first opens, it follows a special timeline.

| Moment | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Registration of the Strata Plan** | The Owners Corporation is born. The Developer holds all votes (they own all unsold lots). |
| **Initial Period** | Until 1/3 of lots are sold to outside buyers. The Developer cannot make big decisions about Common Property. |
| **First AGM** | Within 2 months of the end of the Initial Period. Owners elect the first Strata Committee. The Developer hands over keys, plans, the Strata Roll, the manuals, and the maintenance schedule. |
| **Building Bond (only for Class 2 buildings 4+ storeys not covered by Home Building Compensation insurance)** | Developer lodges 2% of the contract price with NSW Fair Trading. |
| **Inspections** | An independent **Building Inspector** does an interim inspection (15–18 months after completion) and a final inspection (21–24 months). Defects identified must be rectified. |
| **6-Year Major / 2-Year Minor Defects** | Statutory warranties under the **Home Building Act 1989** s.18B run from completion. |
| **DBP Statutory Duty of Care** | The designer, builder, manufacturer and supplier owe a **statutory duty of care** to every owner — current AND future — for **10 years** from completion (DBP Act s.37). This is retrospective: it applies to buildings completed since 11 June 2010. |

> **Rule in one line:** Year 1–2 = developer hand-over and bond. Year 0–10 = DBP duty of care. Year 0–6 = HBA major defects warranty.
> **The law:** *SSMA 2015* Part 3, *SSDA 2015*, *DBP Act 2020* s.37, *HBA 1989* s.18B and Part 11.

# CHAPTER 11 — How to raise an issue (without making it personal)

The law gives you a clear ladder. Climb one rung at a time.

```
  1. Speak to your Strata Committee Secretary
       or your Strata Manager (in writing).
                    │
                    ▼
  2. Strata Committee tries to resolve it
     within their delegated authority.
                    │
        ┌───────────┴───────────┐
     Resolved                Not resolved
        │                       │
        ▼                       ▼
       Done.        3. Raise it as a Motion
                    at the next General Meeting
                    of the Owners Corporation.
                                │
                    ┌───────────┴───────────┐
                Motion passes           Motion fails / dispute
                    │                       │
                    ▼                       ▼
                  Done.        4. FREE MEDIATION
                               with NSW Fair Trading
                               (most disputes require
                               mediation BEFORE NCAT).
                                            │
                                ┌───────────┴───────────┐
                            Resolved              Not resolved
                                │                       │
                                ▼                       ▼
                              Done.        5. Apply to NCAT
                                           (Strata & Community
                                           Schemes Division).
                                           NCAT can order works,
                                           orders, money, by-laws.
```

> **Rule in one line:** Always escalate one step at a time. Mediation is free. NCAT is the final step.
> **The law:** *SSMA 2015* Part 12, NCAT *Civil and Administrative Tribunal Act 2013*.

# CHAPTER 12 — The 7 Mistakes Owners Make (and how to avoid them)

| # | The mistake | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "It's my apartment, I can do what I want." | Read your Strata Plan + by-laws first. Most things touch Common Property somewhere. |
| 2 | "The Strata Manager said it's fine, so it's fine." | The Strata Manager has NO authority to approve remedial works. Only the OC does. Get it in writing in meeting minutes. |
| 3 | "It's just waterproofing, we don't need a designer." | Waterproofing on common property = DBP territory. Skip a Registered Design Practitioner = up to $165,000 fine. |
| 4 | "We don't need a planning consultant, it's just a balcony." | Adding to a balcony, removing planters, replacing balustrades almost always needs DA approval AND DBP compliance. |
| 5 | "Let's pay the builder cash and skip the Portal." | The Building Compliance Declaration is the only document that closes the case. No Portal = no closure = no future buyer will touch the building. |
| 6 | "The defects are old, no point chasing now." | DBP statutory duty of care = 10 years. HBA major defects = 6 years. Both are still alive for most buildings completed since 2010. |
| 7 | "We voted to fix it last year — that's enough." | An OC resolution older than 12 months may need to be re-voted. Always check before signing. |

# CHAPTER 13 — The 30-Second Glossary

- **OC** — Owners Corporation. The "company" of all owners.
- **SC** — Strata Committee. The 3–9 owners elected to do day-to-day work.
- **SM / BM** — Strata Manager / Building Manager. Hired professionals.
- **Common Property** — Everything outside the Lot line. Owned by all owners together.
- **Lot Property** — Everything inside the Lot line. Owned by you alone.
- **By-law** — A rule that binds every owner, registered against the Strata Plan.
- **Strata Plan (SP)** — The legal map showing every Lot and the Common Property.
- **BMS / SMS** — Building / Strata Management Statement. Governs shared facilities in mixed-use buildings.
- **BMC** — Building Management Committee. Manages shared facilities between strata schemes and other lots (e.g. retail).
- **DA / CDC / Exempt** — Three planning approval pathways under the EP&A Act + Codes SEPP.
- **DBP / RAB** — The two 2020 Acts that brought practitioner registration and rectification powers.
- **CIRD** — Construction-Issued Regulated Design.
- **DCD** — Design Compliance Declaration.
- **BCD** — Building Compliance Declaration.
- **PC / OC (the other one)** — Practical Completion / Occupation Certificate.
- **DLP** — Defects Liability Period (usually 12 months, contractual).
- **NCAT** — NSW Civil and Administrative Tribunal.
- **NCC / BCA** — National Construction Code / Building Code of Australia.

# THE 1-PAGE CHEAT SHEET (print this)

### Whose problem is it?

| The thing | Lot or Common? | Who pays? |
|---|---|---|
| Light bulb in your kitchen | LOT | YOU |
| Dishwasher leak | LOT | YOU |
| Original bathroom tiles cracked | COMMON | OC |
| New bathroom tiles you laid leak | LOT | YOU |
| Original waterproofing failed | COMMON | OC |
| External window is broken | COMMON | OC |
| Internal wall fell | LOT (not on plan) / COMMON (on plan) | Check the plan |
| Roof is leaking | COMMON | OC |
| Pipe inside wall, serves only your unit | LOT | YOU |
| Pipe inside wall, serves multiple units | COMMON | OC |
| Front door of your apartment | COMMON (it's on the plan) | OC |
| Lock on your front door | LOT (you can change with notice) | YOU |
| Carpet in your apartment | LOT | YOU |
| Carpet in the corridor | COMMON | OC |

### Before you start ANY work, ask 4 questions:

1. Does it touch Common Property? → Need OC approval.
2. Does it change appearance, structure, fire, or waterproofing? → Need a by-law (special resolution).
3. Does it involve waterproofing or cladding on a Class 2 building? → Need a Registered Design Practitioner + Registered Building Practitioner.
4. Does it need a DA or CDC? → Engage a Planning Consultant.

If yes to ANY of 1–3 → it's not a DIY job.
