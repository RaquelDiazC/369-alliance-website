## Slide 2

Remedial building work
What is remedial building work?
Remedial building work is a broad term to define work involving alterations or additions, or the repair, renovation or protective treatment of an existing building.
Who needs to know?
As the owners corporation, strata committee, strata manager or building manager you will need to be familiar with the obligations relating to remedial building work in strata buildings, and the relevant legislation and regulatory requirements to support and streamline project planning in promoting a successful outcome for remediation projects.

## Slide 3

CLASSES - Strata buildings are typically class 2 buildings, including buildings that contain a class 2 part. While class 3 and 9c buildings are regulated buildings in NSW, they are generally not strata buildings.
Class 2 buildings are residential apartment buildings and are usually multi-storey, multi-unit apartment buildings where people live above and below each other.
The Building Code of Australia (BCA) defines them as a building containing two or more sole-occupancy units, each being a separate dwelling.
Types of class 2 buildings include:
Multi-unit, multi-storey residential apartments
A house with a self-contained granny flat on a second storey
Two single storey dwellings that are attached and have a common space below, such as a carpark or basement.
Class 3 buildings are residential buildings, other than a class 1 or 2 building, that are a common place for long-term or transient living for several unrelated persons.
This includes:
Boarding houses
Hostels
Backpackers accommodation
Residential part of a hotel, motel, school or detention centre
Dormitory style accommodation
Care facilities for the elderly which are not considered class 9.
Class 9c buildings are residential care buildings that may contain residents who have various care level needs.
They are a place of residence where 10% or more of persons who reside there need physical assistance in conducting their daily activities and evacuating the building during an emergency.
An aged care building, where residents are provided with personal care services, is a class 9c building.

## Slide 6

Remedial building work (REM) and exempt development
Under the State Environmental Planning Policy (Exempt and Complying Development Codes) 2008 (Codes SEPP) some minor building alterations and remedial building works do not need any planning or building approval.
Low impact development
If remedial building work does not need planning approval, then this work is referred to as exempt development.
Exempt development is very low impact development that can be done for certain residential, commercial and industrial building.
Remedial building work
Remedial building work covers a wide range of repair and maintenance activities, such as waterproofing, re-tiling, concrete and masonry repairs, window replacement, lintel replacement, re-roofing, and framing member replacements.
Minor building alterations are considered exempt development under the Codes SEPP if that work:
Does not permanently alter the load-bearing capacity and does not reduce the structural adequacy of that building element.
Re-instates a building element to its intended structural capacity and does not reduce the structural adequacy of the building element.
Does not change the original architecture or design intent i.e. ‘like-for-like’.
Poses minimal impacts to the built environment.

Remedial building work (REM) and exempt development
Additional requirements
Note: To be exempt development for the purposes of the Codes SEPP, the remedial building work being carried out:
Must meet the relevant fire safety requirements.
Must meet the relevant deemed-to-satisfy provisions of the Building Code of Australia (BCA), or if there are no relevant provisions, must be structurally adequate.
Must not be in a heritage building.
Must not cause the building to contravene the Building Code of Australia (BCA), where the work is in an existing building.
Must not be in a heritage building.
Must not cause the building to contravene the Building Code of Australia (BCA), where the work is in an existing building.

## Slide 8

Examples of exempt development
Some examples of works considered to be ‘exempt development’ under the Codes SEPP, provided the remedial building work meets the legislative requirements.
External windows and doors - External window and door replacement.
Balcony balustrades - Balcony balustrade repair or replacement.
Roof tiling and wall cladding - Roof tiling, sheeting and wall cladding repair or replacement, provided the tiling materials meets the performance requirements and do not compromise the structural integrity of the building.
Cavity flashing - Replacement of a deteriorated cavity flashing or a leaking cavity flashing, provided the cavity flashing material meets the performance requirements and does not permanently alter the load-bearing elements of the building.
Balcony or terrace paving - Replacement of a tiled balcony or terrace with a pedestal paving system, provided the remedial building work is structurally adequate.
Lintel replacement - Replacement of a steel lintel with a reinforced concrete lintel, provided the remedial building work is supervised by a registered professional engineer, the new lintel is structurally adequate and does not undermine the structural integrity of the building.
Beam, slab or column repairs - Concrete repairs to a beam, slab or column, provided the remedial building work is supervised by a registered professional engineer and the building element is returned to its appropriate structural capacity and does not reduce the structural adequacy, or fire resistance, of that building element.
Balcony, terrace or roof waterproofing - Balcony, terrace or roof waterproofing, provided the waterproofing design complies with the National Construction Code (NCC), Australian Standards and the requirements under the DBP legislation.
Façade repainting - Repainting of façades.

## Slide 14

WATERPROOFING
Waterproofing work will require a registered design practitioner to produce declared regulated designs unless it is specifically excluded work under clause 13 of the DBP Regulation.
Waterproofing is only excluded from the DBP legislation if it meets each of the following:
1
1. The work must be carried out because of alterations to a bathroom, kitchen, laundry, or toilet, and
2. The work, including the agreement to carry out the work, only relates to a single dwelling, and
3
3. It must be an exempt development.

---
## Slide 15

EXTERNAL CLADDING
External cladding work will require a registered design practitioner to produce declared regulated designs unless it is specifically excluded work under clause 13 of the DBP Regulation.
External cladding work is only excluded from the DBP legislation if it is not being completed to comply with a council order or development control order. If a council order or development control order has been issued for the repair, rectification or replacement of external cladding of a building, then the work will require a registered design practitioner to produce declared regulated designs.
ImportantFor remedial building work that is exempt development under the Codes SEPP, generally, the DBP legislation does not apply. However, waterproofing work and external cladding work are exceptions.

## Slide 17

Work that requires planning approval
Some class 2, 3 and 9c remedial building work projects require planning approval, such as a Development Consent or Complying Development Certificate. If the remedial building work commenced after 1 July 2021 for class 2 buildings, or 3 July 2023 for class 3 and 9c buildings, and the project requires planning approval, it is required to comply with both the DBP legislation and the RAB Act.
For exampleA balcony project requiring the removal of planter boxes, which increases the size of the balcony and a replacement of the non-compliant timber balustrade to glass balustrade will likely require planning approval.
If you are unsure if the remedial building work requires planning approval, the owners corporation can engage with a planning consultant to provide advice.
Authorised officers
Authorised officers within Building Commission NSW focus on ensuring that design and building practitioners are registered and that registered design practitioners have prepared declared regulated designs with the necessary details to produce remedial building work that will comply with the Building Code of Australia.
Remedial building work will also be inspected to ensure compliance with the Building Code of Australia, relevant Australian Standards and other relevant Standards.

## Slide 20

Emergency remedial building work
There are some circumstances when urgent rectification work is required to address significant habitability and/or safety issues (otherwise referred to as a ‘reasonable excuse’).
Owners corporations
The DBP legislation provides an opportunity for owners corporations to immediately address the issue, without declared regulated designs.
There are obligations owners corporations must understand if they are planning to proceed with emergency remedial building work, when the DBP legislation applies.
Key criteria
Immediate action is necessary to remedy an issue, and
The issue is causing, or is likely to cause, damage to the building, and:
the ability to inhabit or use the building (or part of the building) for its intended purpose, or
a risk to health or safety, or
a risk of further damage to the building (or part of the building), and
These impacts, or likely impacts, are serious in nature, and
The work undertaken is limited to what is necessary to mitigate these impacts or likely impacts until further remedial building work can be undertaken.

---
## Slide 21

Obligations for emergency remedial building work
For remedial building work where the DBP legislation applies, an owners corporation must engage a registered building practitioner to determine if a 'reasonable excuse' applies to the remedial building work for it be considered emergency remedial building work.
Registered building practitioner
If the work is emergency remedial building work, and the DBP legislation applies, the registered building practitioner can carry out the work without a regulated declared design.
However, the registered building practitioner is required to register the emergency remedial building work on the NSW Planning Portal and complete a building compliance declaration. These documents must be uploaded to the NSW Planning Portal within 7 days of completing the emergency remedial building work.
If the building work does not comply with the Building Code of Australia, the registered building practitioner must communicate this to the owners corporation and indicate this on the building compliance declaration form.

---
## Slide 22

Work that is not emergency remedial building work
Remedial building work is not considered emergency remedial building work if:
1
1. The work undertaken is designed to address the fundamental or underlying cause of the issue, and
2. Immediate action is not necessary to remedy an existing issue before it causes serious damage or further serious damage, or poses a serious impact relating to habitability, health and safety, and
3
3. It is possible for statutory obligations (including obligations under, but not limited to, the DBP legislation, Strata Scheme Management Act and the Environmental Planning and Assessment Act) to be met prior to any serious damage or further serious damage being caused to the building and there is no serious impact relating to habitability, health and safety.

## Slide 29

Practitioner registration – Professional engineer
The DBP legislation requires that design practitioners, building practitioners and professional engineers must be registered to do regulated building work on regulated buildings.
Do all engineers need to be registered?
Not all engineers need to be registered to complete work on regulated buildings. Unregistered engineers can complete work on regulated buildings provided they are working under the supervision of an engineer who is registered as a professional engineer under the design and building practitioners scheme.
The professional engineer registration only allows the engineer to complete or supervise engineering work on a regulated building.
They must also be a registered design practitioner if they intend to complete design compliance declaration forms.

---
## Slide 30

Practitioner registration – design practitioner
Registered design practitioner
A registered design practitioner is responsible for preparing construction issued regulated designs and design compliance declarations (referred to in this module as declared regulated designs).
In other words, the registered design practitioner declares that regulated designs comply with the Building Code of Australia and other relevant standards.

---
## Slide 31

Practitioner registration – building practitioner
A registered building practitioner is someone who agrees to oversee remedial building work. Where there is more than one builder, the registered building practitioner is the principal contractor.
The role is responsible for building in accordance with the declared regulated designs and making building compliance declarations.
In other words, they declare that building work has been constructed in accordance with declared regulated designs and in compliance with the Building Code of Australia.
Can they delegate?
Registered building practitioners are responsible for uploading and lodging documents on the NSW Planning Portal.
The registered building practitioner can delegate the upload and lodgement of designs to an ‘appropriate practitioner’ on the NSW Planning Portal. The appropriate practitioner must be a registered principal design practitioner or a registered design practitioner for the remedial building work.
However, the final building compliance declaration must be made by the registered building practitioner and cannot be delegated.

---
## Slide 32

Construction-issued regulated designs
A registered design practitioner must prepare construction-issued regulated designs and the registered building practitioner must build according to these.
A construction-issued regulated design is a design, including a plan, specification or a report detailing a design, that is prepared for a building element, or for a performance solution, for building work.
Purpose
A construction-issued regulated design must contain the necessary detail to produce building work that would achieve compliance with the Building Code of Australia and can be used by a building practitioner to carry out the work in accordance with the regulated design and the Building Code of Australia.
Designs for building elements are considered important as they are likely to have a greater impact on the safety and quality of construction in a building.

## Slide 33

Suggested additions
The following information is also suggested for design practitioners when preparing construction issued regulated designs for remedial building work. Including this information is intended to benefit both practitioners and building owners.
These additional drawings may be produced as digital drawings, marked up photos, sketches or pictorial diagrams.
General elevations
‘General elevations’ to identify the location of remedial work in the context of the overall building (for example, the specific balconies or windows to be worked on).
General sections
‘General sections’ to give context and measurements (unless sufficient details and measurements are provided in detailed construction sections).

## Slide 35

The nsw planning portal
What is the NSW Planning Portal?
The NSW Planning Portal is a New South Wales Government initiative that provides a public online platform for digital planning services. It aims to promote legislative requirements under the Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979, Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020 and Residential Apartment Buildings (Compliance and Enforcement Powers) Act 2020.
The NSW Planning Portal is the legal repository for planning and building documents, such as Ministerial Orders, and houses a range of digital planning services, mapping tools and reporting tools that cover the entire planning lifecycle.
Lodging declared regulated designs
The registered building practitioner needs to use the NSW Planning Portal when they are completing regulated building work on a class 2, 3 or 9c building or a building with a class 2, 3 or 9c part (regulated buildings).
For remedial building work which is considered exempt development, there are three stages when action needs to be taken by the registered building practitioner on the NSW Planning Portal.

## Slide 36

remedial building work starts
Stage 1: Before remedial building work starts
Declared regulated designs, prepared by a registered design practitioner, must be provided to the registered building practitioner and lodged on the NSW Planning Portal before the regulated building work starts, except in the case of emergency remedial building work.
The nominated registered building practitioner is responsible for uploading and lodgement of the declared regulated designs on the NSW Planning Portal. The NSW Planning Portal produces lodgement receipts which can be provided to the owners corporation by the registered building practitioner.
Stage 2: During remedial building work
Where variations to the declared regulated designs are required, the owners corporation is expected to obtain variations to the declared regulated designs prepared by a registered design practitioner, for lodgement on the NSW Planning Portal.
The registered building practitioner is responsible for the lodgement of the varied declared regulated designs.
Stage 3: On completion of the remedial building work
At the point of project completion, the registered building practitioner must complete the building compliance declaration on the NSW Planning Portal.
This includes lodging the contractor document and a variation statement (where applicable) as part of the building compliance declaration.

## Slide 37

Before remedial building work starts
When remedial building work is required, which is considered exempt development and where the DBP legislation applies, a registered building practitioner can initiate and submit a remedial works application on the NSW Planning Portal.
•The application will require them to upload and lodge declared regulated designs.
•The application captures information about the remedial building work, including but not limited to, the building work commencement date, the project value and a description of the remedial building work planned.
•The registered building practitioner is also required to upload the contractor’s public liability and contract works insurances and confirm when the regulated remedial building work is scheduled to commence.

## Slide 38

Before remedial building work starts
Waterproofing scenario: Before the work starts
3. The process
The process of complying with the Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020 on the NSW Planning Portal, starts with upload and lodgement of declared regulated designs.
4. Required documents
Documents can be progressively uploaded but must be lodged as a complete set before the regulated remedial building work can commence.
This includes:
The construction-issued regulated design/s that the registered building practitioner will use to do the remedial building work (in this case, for waterproofing).
The design compliance declaration prepared by the registered design practitioner who prepared the construction-issued regulated design.
5. Declared regulated designs
The registered design practitioner needs to declare that regulated designs comply with the Building Code of Australia and other standards, codes or requirements.
In this example, the declared regulated design relates to the rooftop waterproofing.
The declaration is completed using a design compliance declaration form.
6. Work according to the declared regulated design
The registered building practitioner must work according to the declared regulated design.
7. Lodgement on the NSW Planning Portal
The construction-issued regulated designs and design compliance declaration forms must be uploaded and lodged on the NSW Planning Portal by the registered building practitioner or nominated appropriate practitioner before the regulated remedial building work can commence on replacing the waterproofing membrane.

during remedial building work
Sometimes during remedial building work, the declared regulated designs need to be changed. This is called a variation.
•When a variation is required, a varied construction issued regulated design, and a design compliance declaration must be prepared by the registered design practitioner.
•The registered building practitioner must lodge the varied declared regulated designs on the NSW Planning Portal.
Waterproofing scenario: During the work
Something's different
Two weeks into the replacement of the rooftop waterproofing membrane, the registered building practitioner identifies that a section of the rooftop requires a slightly different waterproofing approach to the declared regulated designs.
Variation required
The registered design practitioner considers the required variation and communicates with the owners corporation about the need for a variation to the declared regulated design and seeks approval for the additional work to be carried out.

Lodgement on the NSW Planning Portal

## Slide 40

completion remedial building work
When the remedial building work is completed, the registered building practitioner must complete and lodge the building compliance declaration on the NSW Planning Portal. Only the registered building practitioner can lodge a building compliance declaration. This task cannot be delegated.

Some remedial building projects may have more than one variation to lodge. The registered design practitioner prepares the variation for the regulated design, where required.
Contractor documentA statement of the contractors who were engaged to complete the regulated building work (i.e. waterproofing, in the case of exempt development remedial building work).
